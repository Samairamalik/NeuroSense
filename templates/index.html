Method and limitations

How this works

Four models across three modalities. Each is scored independently — none of them sees the others' output.

  • Handwriting
    Input
    Spiral and wave drawings, one image each.
    Method
    Grayscale, resized to 200×200, inverted Otsu threshold, then a 27,648-dimensional HOG descriptor — histograms of edge orientation per 8×8 cell.
    Model
    Two RBF support vector machines, one per drawing type. Their outputs are averaged.
    Limits
    Trained on a few hundred drawings. Sensitive to how the drawing was photographed or scanned.
  • MRI scan
    Input
    One brain MRI slice.
    Method
    Grayscale, resized to 128×128, scaled to [0,1], flattened to 16,384 pixel features.
    Model
    RBF support vector machine.
    Limits
    Flattening discards all spatial structure, so results depend heavily on slice position, orientation and contrast. This is the weakest of the three designs — treat its output with the most caution.
  • Voice
    Input
    A short recording; a sustained “ahh” works best.
    Method
    Mono at 16 kHz, silence trimmed, then 15 selected features — predominantly MFCC standard deviations, read from the model's own record of its training columns.
    Model
    Random forest, 100 trees, maximum depth 8.
    Limits
    The training script for this model is not in the repository, so the exact training-time definitions of jitter, shimmer and HNR could not be verified. Absolute probabilities should be treated with particular caution.
  • Combining modalities
    Method
    A weighted mean over whichever modalities you supplied, currently equally weighted.
    Limits
    The per-modality probabilities are not calibrated against each other. An SVM's 0.70 and a random forest's 0.70 do not carry the same meaning, so the combined figure is indicative only. Always read the individual modalities first.

On confidence. These models output a single probability with no calibrated uncertainty interval, so this interface does not draw one. Instead, any result falling between 40% and 60% is reported as inconclusive, because a value that close to the decision threshold does not support a conclusion in either direction.
